BANGLADESH ARRIVAL GATEWAY
Global benchmark and Bangladesh source register

Prepared: 21 August 2026
Scope: Bangladesh current state, policy and opportunity; global benchmark and cross-cutting standards

Method

- Priority was given to government, intergovernmental and official industry-body sources.
- “Fact used” records what the source can safely support.
- Design implications are explicitly marked as interpretation rather than source fact.
- Product rules and webpages can change. Recheck all operational details immediately before the final presentation is issued.
- Bangladesh operational webpages, statistics and policy records are included. System status and legal interpretation still require official stakeholder and counsel validation.


S01 — ICAO: API Guidelines and PNR Reporting Standards

Issuer: International Civil Aviation Organization
URL: https://www.icao.int/facilitation-programmes/api-guidelines-and-pnr-reporting-standards
Accessed: 21 August 2026
Fact used: Modern air-border control relies substantially on secure electronic data; API is produced at check-in and PNR originates in booking records; advance data enables risk-based targeted controls before arrival.
Caveat: API/PNR are airline-to-government data streams, not the same as a traveller-completed arrival declaration.
Confidence: High.


S02 — WCO/IATA/ICAO: Guidelines on Advance Passenger Information (2022)

Issuer: WCO, IATA and ICAO; hosted by ICAO
URL: https://www.icao.int/Security/FAL/Documents/1.%20API%20Guidelines.EN%202022.final.pdf
Accessed: 21 August 2026
Fact used: ICAO Annex 9 requires a Passenger Data Single Window for required API/iAPI and/or PNR categories, using a common transmission entry point; it also addresses 24/7 support, outage recovery and standardized data.
Design implication: A national arrival programme should align with the wider passenger-data framework and avoid creating another isolated agency silo.
Confidence: High.


S03 — ICAO Facilitation Manual, Chapter 9: Passenger data exchange systems

SOURCE USE RULES FOR THE PRESENTATION

1. Cite the issuing body and year in the slide footnote and retain the direct URL in speaker notes.
2. Never turn a qualitative purpose statement such as “reduce time” into an invented percentage.
3. Distinguish traveller counts, tourist arrivals, passenger journeys, RPK demand and declaration transactions.
4. Label Cambodia's number as public accesses, not completed declarations.
5. Label UN Tourism's January 2025 figure as the cited estimate and refresh it before publication.
6. Treat source-reported benefits as evidence of intent unless an audited outcome measure is available.
7. Do not imply that API/PNR and a traveller declaration are interchangeable.
8. Recheck all operational rules, submission windows and coverage before final delivery.
9. Bangladesh law and local statistics must be sourced from official bodies and legally reviewed where interpretation is required.
10. Separate source facts from our strategic recommendations in both slide text and speaker notes.
11. Do not describe Tourifye as replacing or owning the existing E-Arrival Card; describe an integration, evolution or coordinated-service proposal.
12. Treat NBR milestone dates as plans unless completion is independently confirmed by the responsible authority.
13. Never compare the 660,000 foreign-tourist-arrival series directly with the 7.92 million international-passenger figure without explaining the different year and population.
14. Reverify Third Terminal operating status immediately before publication.
